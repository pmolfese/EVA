title: Frontal sweat simulation, linked GSR, and automatic wavelet channel health
---

This release expands Simulator Studio with larger, slower sweat artifacts,
geometry-aware frontal placement, and an optional GSR channel linked to the
same episodes. Channel Health now includes wavelet burden by default, with
clearer goodness scores and easier access to its settings.

## Larger, slower sweat artifacts

**Simulator Studio > Generate > Muscle** now exposes sweat episode rate,
drift amplitude, duration, and channels per episode directly in the GUI.
The default nominal drift increases from 100 µV over 15 seconds to **500 µV
over 30 seconds**. Individual episodes still vary in amplitude, duration,
and polarity.

- **Large, widespread shifts** sets 1,500 µV, six episodes per minute,
  30-second durations, and up to eight electrodes.
- **Allow overlapping shifts** lets new episodes begin while earlier ones
  are active, so long durations no longer have to limit the requested rate.
- **Vary amplitude and polarity by channel** gives electrodes different
  contributions rather than an identical common shift that can disappear
  under average referencing.

Amplitude remains adjustable **before referencing**. Average referencing can
produce smaller shifts in channels outside the directly affected electrodes;
the saved truth identifies where the artifact was actually injected.

## Frontal placement and editable electrode selection

New sweat configurations default to **Frontal-biased clusters**. Cluster
centers prefer frontal electrodes, neighboring electrodes are favored, and
drift strength tapers toward the edges. Both **Frontal preference** and
**Cluster spread** are editable; their defaults are 85% and 35°.

The placement selector also offers **Frontal only**, **Anywhere**, and
**Selected electrodes**, with GUI checkboxes for the eligible channel
numbers. Frontal classification uses electrode coordinates rather than names
or channel order, supporting numbered channels, dense arrays, and imported
montages. Frontal-only and selected modes cap each episode at the available
eligible electrodes and report when fewer qualify than requested; an empty
eligible set is rejected.

Older scenarios without placement settings retain the original Anywhere
behavior for reproducibility. The new controls are also available through
EVASimulate's command-line options and saved scenario files.

## Optional GSR channel linked to sweat

**GSR channel (linked to sweat)** adds a separate auxiliary conductance trace
in **µS**. Its positive responses follow the same episode onsets, durations,
and smooth envelopes as the EEG sweat drift, scale with absolute episode
amplitude, and add when episodes overlap. EEG polarity, spatial placement,
and referencing do not change how GSR is derived from those episodes.

Baseline conductance and nominal response are editable in the GUI, defaulting
to **2 µS** and **1 µS**. Enabling GSR also enables default sweat when needed;
turning sweat off afterward leaves a flat conductance baseline. Turning GSR
off leaves the EEG samples unchanged. GSR is a controlled synthetic proxy,
rather than an independently validated physiological sensor model.

MFF import and export now preserve per-channel auxiliary units, so GSR
remains conductance rather than being relabeled as voltage. The CLI provides
matching GSR enable/disable, baseline, and response options.

## Automatic wavelet scoring in Channel Health

Wavelet artifact burden now runs automatically with **Channel Health /
Goodness**, unless disabled in **Channel Goodness Settings**. The preference
is saved, defaults to enabled for existing settings, and preserves previously
customized wavelet parameters. **Channels > Health > Run Wavelets** also
provides a manual run beside Refresh. Automatic health refreshes recompute
the base metrics along with wavelet burden.

Metric percentages now explicitly say **% good**, and weakest-metric
summaries provide more room and hover text. Kurtosis details include the
measured excess kurtosis and its good/poor thresholds. Preferences and
Channel Goodness Settings have more vertical space, and wavelet settings
scroll so the controls remain accessible.

## Verification

Simulator regression checks now cover saved sweat waveforms and duration
markers, overlapping and spatially varied episodes, frontal placement and
cluster spread, electrode reordering and renaming, restricted selection, and
legacy scenario compatibility. GSR checks cover episode alignment, auxiliary
units through MFF import/export, and identical EEG with GSR enabled or
disabled. Additional Channel Health tests cover automatic-wavelet preference
persistence, migration of older settings, and kurtosis goodness scoring.
