This release fixes event alignment in continuous MFF recordings with acquisition
pauses and adds a Decision Matrix sort that groups rejected epochs together.

## Correct event timing after MFF acquisition pauses

Some MFF recordings contain separate acquisition intervals because saving EEG
was paused and resumed—for example, by experiment-control software. The signal
blocks omit the paused time, while event timestamps include it. Previously,
EVA joined those blocks without restoring the elapsed time, placing later event
markers too late relative to the EEG. This could shift PSA epochs, their
baselines, and MRI correction triggers even when the events occurred long after
the pause. With multiple pauses, the discrepancy accumulated.

EVA now preserves the acquisition timeline declared in **epochs.xml**. Missing
intervals receive zero placeholders and a duration-bearing **Acquisition gap**
event (`BAD_ACQ_SKIP`). Those zeros represent missing data, not measured EEG.
Event timestamps remain on the original recording clock, keeping later markers
aligned with their samples.

- **Filtering:** the main filter processes recorded intervals independently,
  including adaptive line-noise reduction, without filtering across a gap.
- **PSA:** epochs overlapping a gap are excluded even when artifact rejection
  is off or decisions are deferred to the Decision Matrix. A missing interval
  cannot be rescued by interpolating channels or manually keeping the trial.
- **MRI correction:** correction stops with an explanatory message if a gap
  intersects its trigger-window support. Process uninterrupted intervals
  separately in that case. A pause well before or after that support does not
  block correction.
- **PNS and export:** physiological channels retain their acquisition timing,
  and continuous MFF export preserves the separate acquisition intervals for
  reopening and sharing. Gap markers are protected from event-anchor preference
  rules, including wildcard rules.

Uninterrupted recordings retain their sample values and timing. Intentionally
segmented and averaged MFF files keep their existing epoch layout. Inconsistent
acquisition metadata or excessive gap-padding requests are refused rather than
silently creating a misleading timeline.

**For affected recordings, reopen the original MFF and rebuild the processing,
epochs, and averages.** Already generated outputs are not corrected automatically.
An older export that discarded the original acquisition timing cannot recover it
just by reopening. Successful MRI artifact removal alone does not establish that
task-event alignment was correct.

## Group rejected epochs in the Decision Matrix

Choose **Epochs → Rejected first** to place all rejected epochs before the kept
epochs. The sort uses the final review decision, including artifact rules,
interpolation feasibility, health thresholds, and manual choices. Within each
group, lower Segment Health scores come first; acquisition order breaks ties.
The order updates when review decisions or thresholds change.

**Epoch goodness** remains available for sorting by Segment Health alone.
Because the red epoch indicator means rejection for any reason, red epochs can
be interspersed with kept epochs in that mode. Hover help explains the difference.
Sorting changes display order without changing trial identities or decisions.

## MRI slice-timing feedback

Slice-based MRI correction now shows an advisory comparison between the configured
acquisition-group interval and a strong repeat measured across sampled scan windows
and channels. It also warns when fractional sample timing lacks interpolation or
sub-sample alignment. The same assessment is saved with the run's health metrics
and export log. It does not block processing, infer anatomical slice count, or
replace the existing output-residue checks; inconclusive evidence is identified.
FASTR's donor count is now labeled **Donor Epochs**, and Allen AAS's uniform epoch
count is labeled **Groups / volume**, to distinguish acquisition groups from
anatomical slices and complete TRs. Saved settings retain their existing meaning.

## Verification

Focused MRI timing, run-grade and settings validation passed **45 tests** across
three suites. The timing probe was also checked on two real recordings: it flags
11 or 22 configured groups against the measured ~87 ms repeat and agrees with
the confirmed 23-group acquisition, while identifying missing fractional timing.

The acquisition-gap fix passed **51 regression tests** across eight suites,
covering uninterrupted recordings, multiple gaps, PNS alignment, export/reopening,
event-time precision, segmented and averaged files, PSA exclusions, interval-based
filtering, and event-anchor rules. Separate Decision Matrix tests cover grouping
and reordering after manual, health-threshold, and interpolation decisions.

On a real MRI recording with a 465 ms pause, the fixed reader matched MNE's
452,363-sample timeline and all 205 MRI marker times. Checked EEG samples agreed
on that timeline, and the regenerated Shape and Face averages reproduced the
independently gap-corrected reference within 0.000004 µV. This validates the timing
repair; it is not a claim that those exploratory averages are free of other
artifacts.
