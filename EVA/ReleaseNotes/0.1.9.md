## New: MAAC artifact-correction workflows

EVA now provides dedicated, reviewable workflows for the first four matched
corrections in the Multi-Algorithm Artifact Correction (MAAC) procedure. Each
stage separates analysis from application: EVA shows the evidence and proposed
correction first, then adds a definition to **Clean Artifacts** for an explicit,
reversible apply step.

### MAAC-1: saccadic spike potential

**Artifacts > Saccadic Spike Potential…** detects the brief biphasic voltage
transient at saccade onset in the Cz-referenced first-difference domain. EVA
proposes Cz, vertical-EOG, lower-EOG, and horizontal-EOG channel roles; reports
preliminary and confirmed candidates; and shows the scalp template before it is
accepted.

The recommended mode maps a bundled 33-channel canonical template onto the
recording montage. A session-average alternative is available when that map is
not appropriate. Confirmed events use the dedicated **SP Spatial Filter**, which
fits and removes only the saved spike topography inside each short event window.

### MAAC-2: corneo-retinal dipole

**Artifacts > Corneo-Retinal Dipole…** estimates the continuous scalp field
associated with horizontal and vertical eye position. A specialized preliminary
blink scan creates an editable estimation mask; masked predictor samples are
interpolated so blinks do not distort the eye-position maps. EVA then performs
the horizontal followed by vertical reverse-EMCP regressions and presents both
maps, the included blink spans, and removed-signal RMS for review.

**Use MAAC-2 Result** adds the whole-recording correction to **Clean Artifacts**
without applying it immediately. The blink spans remain estimation masks rather
than silently becoming blink corrections or rejected epochs.

### MAAC-3: movement PCA

**Artifacts > Movement Artifact (PCA)…** runs temporal PCA independently within
stored epochs or fixed, non-overlapping continuous windows, followed by a
Varimax start and Promax oblique rotation. EVA back-projects each factor and
marks ranges whose channel peak-to-peak contribution exceeds the configurable
threshold, 200 µV by default.

Analysis now runs independent ranges concurrently within EVA's bounded CPU
budget. Its progress panel identifies the current phase and reports completed
ranges and samples, worker count, flagged and skipped ranges, removed factors,
throughput, elapsed time, and estimated time remaining. Results are restored to
recording order before they are exposed, preserving deterministic output.

The review sheet lists affected ranges and factor amplitudes. **Add Movement
Markers** creates duration-bearing `MOV` markers and a **MAAC Movement**
definition preset to **MAAC-3 Movement PCA**. It closes the review sheet without
opening or running **Clean Artifacts**; applying later reruns the saved,
deterministic configuration. Averaged data are refused, and bad, interpolated,
or unselected channels are left unchanged.

### MAAC-4: muscle BSS-CCA

**Artifacts > Muscle Artifact (BSS-CCA)…** separates muscle-like sources using
canonical correlation between the multichannel signal and its one-sample delay.
Automatic suggestions combine low temporal autocorrelation with the published
1–15 Hz versus 15–30 Hz spectral-power ratio, while retaining an explicit
per-range, per-component review table.

Detection now remains inside the MAAC-4 sheet. Its progress panel explains the
current BSS-CCA stage and reports windows and samples processed, analyzed,
flagged, and skipped ranges, classified and selected components, throughput,
elapsed time, and estimated time remaining. No correction is applied during
this scan.

Affected intervals appear as duration-bearing `EMG` candidates with full-span
waveform highlighting, direct scope buttons, Previous/Next navigation, and
per-component include/exclude controls. **Add Muscle Markers** saves those
reviewed ranges and the deterministic component overrides, then closes MAAC-4;
it does not open or run **Clean Artifacts**. The user opens that sheet later to
preview and apply BSS-CCA explicitly.

Stored epochs are analyzed independently. Continuous recordings use overlapping
10-second windows with smooth crossfades, estimating the CCA operator near 250
Hz and applying it to the native-rate samples. Bands, classifier threshold,
analysis rate, windowing, and every component decision are editable and saved.
Existing ICA/ICLabel Muscle labels appear as a QC cross-check only. EVA refuses
averaged data or insufficient EMG bandwidth and preserves bad, interpolated,
unselected, non-finite, and PNS-only channels.

Standalone Clean Artifacts processing still follows the order in which the
definitions are shown. The cleaning core now also has a stable MAAC-ordering
policy—saccadic spike, corneo-retinal correction, other artifact definitions,
movement, then muscle—as the foundation for a future one-click full-MAAC
pipeline.

## Reorganized Artifacts menu and independent detectors

The **Artifacts** menu is now grouped in workflow order: Clean Artifacts and its
visibility control; Eye Blink, Eye Movement, and Threshold Settings; ICA;
ECG/QRS and BCG; the MAAC tools; and wavelet tools at the bottom.

ICA and threshold detection are no longer mutually exclusive. The old Method
picker and default-method preference have been removed, and **ICA…** is now a
standalone command. Eye Blink and Eye Movement threshold detection can remain
enabled while ICA is run or reviewed; after ICA removal, enabled detectors run
again on the updated signal. Threshold/QRS refreshes replace only the events
they own, preserving unrelated MAAC, BCG, wavelet, and defined-artifact markers.

## New: LAVI and individualized ABBA bands

The waveform EEG menu now opens a standalone **Rhythmicity Explorer** for
recording-level rhythmicity analysis. Bands mode computes lagged phase
persistence (LAVI), discovers individualized sustained and transient ABBA
regions, and presents a selected-channel spectrum alongside sensor-frequency,
gallery, topography, and detection-rate summaries.

The Paper 2026 preset adds a recording-matched 200-surrogate significance
ribbon. An explicitly labeled exploratory mode omits inference. Runs are
cancellable, never publish partial results, exclude bad channels and marked
artifact intervals by default, and retain the prior result with a visible stale
state whenever its signal, selection, channels, or settings no longer match.

Exports are reproducible directory packages containing a versioned JSON
manifest, long-form LAVI and ABBA CSV tables, channel × frequency NPY arrays,
the selected processing history, validity warnings, backend, seed, and pinned
method/reference versions.

An explicit **Use in Time-Frequency** action publishes the displayed channel's
ABBA bands for the open recording session. Time-Frequency provides a band-source
picker, draws display-only sustained/transient borders and peak markers over
ERSP/ITPC maps, uses the chosen ranges for ROI scalars and exports, and records
their full source provenance. Stale results are rejected, applying one channel's
borders to an all-channel overview is stated explicitly, and saved frequency-
band preferences are not modified.

## New: event-related WTPL

The **Event-related** workspace provides Within-Trial Phase Locking (WTPL), with
raw WTPL, explicit baseline-subtracted ΔWTPL, valid-trial support, and A − B
condition maps using the same retained epochs as EVA's ERP and Time-Frequency
views.

WTPL uses signed one-cycle lags and complex-plane fractional interpolation.
Invalid wavelet and lag edges remain visibly hatched NaNs. A requested baseline
must fit completely inside the epoch; EVA reports an unavailable ΔWTPL result
instead of silently shortening the interval.

The Time-Frequency workspace offers WTPL as an optional measure backed by the
same shared engine. Event-related exports include condition-specific raw, delta,
and valid-count NPY maps, band × window scalar CSV, axes, method and processing
provenance, and validity warnings.

The implementation matches a committed, deterministic Python oracle that
independently evaluates the published equation. Tests also pin amplitude
invariance, fractional lags, phase resets, trial boundaries, online averaging,
baseline semantics, condition comparisons, export structure, and the scientific
distinction between WTPL and ITPC.

## New: rhythmic burst analysis

The **Bursts** workspace provides P90 peak detection, P75 or WTPL boundary
refinement, deterministic overlap merging, and optional channel-specific
LAVI/ABBA band assignment. Detections are linked across power or WTPL maps, the
waveform, duration distribution, band rate/occupancy summaries, filters, and a
sortable table.

Reproducible exports include burst and summary CSVs, normalized-power/WTPL NPY
maps, axis sidecars, warnings, and full method/source provenance. Burst
detections describe neural rhythmicity and are explicitly separated from
artifact detection and cleaning; this workspace offers no clean or apply action.

## New: Metal acceleration and saved results

LAVI can use a compiled, standalone Metal shader for frequency-tiled Morlet
coefficients. Automatic mode selects Metal only above a profiled workload
crossover and records the backend and numerical precision actually used.
CPU-only operation remains available in Preferences, and unsupported devices,
memory limits, or GPU failures automatically return to the bounded Double-
precision Accelerate FFT path with a visible warning.

On the reference Apple-Silicon development host, a 32,768-sample 4 Hz tile ran
about 5× faster on Metal. Compiled-kernel tests pin coefficient and LAVI
tolerances, identical valid-pair counts, and exact ABBA band boundaries against
the CPU oracle.

Paper-significance generation now uses a deterministic, memory-bounded CPU
worker pool across surrogates. Accelerate workers own their FFT/IAAFT plans and
run frequencies serially, avoiding nested oversubscription. When Metal is the
resolved backend, IAAFT remains parallel on CPU while bounded surrogate batches
are transformed and reduced by compiled `.metal` kernels; only compact LAVI
profiles return to the CPU. Metal failures still restart safely on the existing
Double-precision Accelerate fallback.

Completed LAVI/ABBA analyses are saved as compact, checksummed payloads keyed to
their recording. Reopening a recording can restore its spectrum, bands,
selection, and provenance without storing EEG samples or surrogate waveforms.
Restored results are current only when the processed-signal revision, selection
and channel context, EVA method version, and pinned upstream reference still
match. Any mismatch is visibly stale and cannot be reused as a current
Time-Frequency band source until the analysis is recomputed.

Exact paper-significance results are also cached independently per channel.
Repeating a matching run—or expanding a completed current-channel run to all
good channels—reuses the completed aperiodic fit, 200-surrogate ribbon, and
convergence diagnostics. Changes to the signal revision, selected segments,
processing context, channel, sampling rate, full analysis configuration, seed,
backend/precision, method version, or pinned upstream reference force a fresh
calculation. Cache payloads are checksummed and never contain EEG samples,
surrogate waveforms, or partial/cancelled work; cache hits are reported in both
progress and the run log.

Long paper-significance runs now report the aperiodic fit, IAAFT workspace
preparation, current surrogate and iteration checkpoint, and surrogate LAVI
profile calculation in the progress area. The run log records the analysis
scope, significance workload, phase changes, surrogate milestones, elapsed
time, and backend actually used, and follows new entries automatically.

## Validation, documentation, and organization

A measured parity study now documents EVA's wavelet reducer against HAPPE 4.1.0
outputs from real 256-channel EEG for the resting-state, task/non-ERP, and ERP
wavelet configurations, with the complete method, numerical summaries, and
comparison figures included in the provenance documentation.

The manual adds an end-to-end **Explore Rhythmicity With Trials And Averages**
tutorial connecting LAVI/ABBA bands, event-related WTPL, rhythmic bursts,
Trials, Averages, and Time-Frequency. Artifact documentation now covers all four
new MAAC workflows, their review steps, ordering, bandwidth constraints, and
interpretation safeguards.

Developer documentation now includes an architecture overview and subsystem
maps for the app, pipeline, analysis, I/O, simulation, and signal-processing
domains. MFF Quick Look sources were consolidated under the shared
`EVAPreviewKit/MFF` organization; this is an internal cleanup with no change to
the previews users receive.

## Interpretation and references

LAVI measures temporal phase persistence, not spectral power or ITPC. WTPL
compares nearby times within individual trials, whereas ITPC compares trials at
the same time and frequency. EVA presents sustained/transient dynamics and
rhythmic bursts as measurements to interpret, not automatic functional diagnoses
or artifact labels.

Methods and validation follow Karvat et al. (2026), *Universal rhythmic
architecture uncovers two modes of neural dynamics*, and the authors' pinned
[LAVI/ABBA toolbox](https://github.com/laaanchic/LAVI).

The MAAC workflows follow Dien (2024),
[*Multi-Algorithm Artifact Correction (MAAC) procedure part one: Algorithm and
example*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2024.108775). MAAC-4's BSS-CCA and
spectral review also draw on [De Clercq et al.
(2006)](https://doi.org/10.1109/TBME.2006.879459) and [De Vos et al.
(2010)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12021-010-9071-0).
