This release brings the simulator into the app as a real window rather than a
separate command-line tool, adds a time-frequency lens alongside the existing
Average/Trials views, teaches EVA to read FIF files natively, and introduces
**EVAResolve**, a companion app for bringing MNE- and OpenMEEG-built head
models into EVA's own forward pipeline.

## New: Simulator Studio and the Source Simulator window

EVASimulate's generation engine and forward model now live inside EVA's own
app module, reachable from two windows:

- **Simulator Studio** (File ▸ New ▸ Simulated Recording) generates a full
  synthetic MFF recording from inside the GUI — per-band background
  amplitudes, coordinate import, and preset scenarios in Generate mode, plus
  Score, Sweep, and Group modes for batch validation work that previously
  meant leaving the app and scripting the CLI by hand.

## New: Event-related time-frequency

A **Time-Frequency** view tab joins Average and Trials, computing ERSP,
inter-trial phase coherence (ITPC), and a DPSS multitaper spectrogram per
epoch — each cross-checked against MNE-Python to better than 1e-6 agreement.
Results export as byte-identical NPY arrays for downstream analysis in Python,
or as a tidy scalar CSV for spreadsheet-style summaries.

## New: native FIF support, and EVAResolve for BEM import

EVA now reads `.fif` files directly — including a Finder QuickLook preview —
without a conversion step.

**EVAResolve** ALPHA/experimental is a new, separate app bundled alongside EVA
(`gov.nih.nimh.cmn.eva.resolve`) whose one job is bringing externally-built
head models into EVA's forward pipeline: import a boundary-element model from
MNE or OpenMEEG, or a finite-element model from DUNEuro, and resolve it into
the same `EVACore/Core/Forward` geometry that EVA and EVASimulate already
share. This is deliberately scoped to import — segmentation, meshing, and
solving stay in the tools that already do that well.

## New: QuickLook previews across more neuroimaging formats

Finder previews and thumbnails now cover GIFTI surface meshes (with an
interactive 3D turntable — drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom), NIfTI and
MGH volumes, DICOM images, and CIFTI connectivity matrices, alongside the
existing MFF and FIF previews.

## Also new

- **EVACore** is now a real shared module: forward models
  (spherical/ellipsoidal/BEM), channel geometry, DSP, and MFF I/O compile
  once and are used by EVA, EVASimulate, and EVAResolve alike, rather than
  each maintaining its own copy.
- **Waveform display scales** — the on-screen amplitude and sweep-speed
  sliders' physical-unit readout is now rate-independent.

## Fixes

- **Single Trial Analysis** No longer crashes or misbehaves when a category
  has no good trials, or fewer than two.
- **Cardiac (BCG) review** Fixed a display issue in the detection panel.
- **Simulator motion-sensor channel** The modelled sensor applied its sigmoid
  gain of 0.1 to a mean BCG in µV, so at the default 100 µV amplitude the
  sigmoid argument reached ±5 and the "saturating nonlinearity" became a
  comparator — 12% of samples pinned to a rail and every beat flattened into a
  square wave. Worse, the severity tracked the BCG amplitude, so a 10-200 µV
  amplitude sweep silently swept how much information the reference channel
  carried. The gain is now dimensionless and applies to the BCG normalized by
  its own RMS, so the peaks compress hard (to 92% of full scale) without ever
  pinning, and identically at every amplitude. The default is 1.0, and shipped
  scenarios were updated; the EEG is byte-identical.

## Simulator

- **Inline explanations.** Options whose label cannot tell you which value to
  pick now carry a `?` button: what the control does, a side-by-side breakdown
  of the choices where there are choices, and — the part that was missing —
  which one to actually use and why. Covers the EEG generator, source depth and
  orientation, head-model shells, reference, lead-field terms, electrode
  jitter, artifact oversampling and anti-aliasing, gradient clock offset and
  slow modulation, the BCG spatial model, the cardiac and ECG knobs, ocular
  topography model, clipping, line noise, impedance, bad channels, sweep
  parameter, and the group-cohort variability settings. Hovering gives the
  one-line summary without a click.
- **Bad channels and high-impedance channels by count.** The Defects tab now
  takes a _number_ of channels to spoil rather than requiring each one to be
  named in a scenario file, with a defect kind (or one of each in turn) and a
  placement. Separately, a number of channels can be given a poor impedance
  reading while their data is left alone — the complement of the flat,
  bridged electrode that reads excellent, and together the pair of cases that
  show impedance is a screen and not a proxy for data quality. Selection is
  seeded, so the same seed always picks the same electrodes, and the two sets
  are always disjoint. `--bad-channel-count`, `--bad-channel-kind`,
  `--high-impedance-count` and `--high-impedance` on the CLI.
- **Eye-electrode defects.** Bad channels can be aimed at the periocular sites
  (`--bad-channel-placement periocular`), identified from the montage's own
  blink topography rather than a list of 10-20 names, so it works on imported
  nets too. This defeats threshold-based blink and eye-movement detection at
  the source: with Fp1/Fp2 flat, the blink drive falls from 105 µV to 1.3 µV
  and every threshold crossing disappears, while the blinks remain in the
  recording and the truth file still lists exactly when they happened. The
  dedicated VEOG/HEOG traces can also be spoiled independently (`--bad-eog
veog:flat,heog:noisy`), which breaks the other thing — regression-based
  ocular correction, which cannot tell a dead reference from an eye that never
  moved. `avoid-periocular` does the opposite, leaving ocular detection intact.
- **Motion sensor channel is now off by default** in Simulator Studio. It is a
  reference channel for BCG-correction research, and a saturating nonlinearity
  is not what most recordings want in the file. The CLI default is unchanged.
- **ECG realism knobs.** _Beat-to-beat variation_ gives every complex its own
  P/QRS/T amplitude and its own PR/QT timing, so no two beats are the same
  stamp — 0.05-0.10 reads as recorded rather than drawn. _Sensor noise_ layers
  broadband electrode and chest-wall EMG noise onto the trace in µV RMS, which
  is what makes R-peak detection a task rather than an argmax. Both default to
  0, so every existing benchmark number is unchanged; `--ecg-morphology-jitter`
  and `--ecg-noise` expose them from the CLI, alongside `--ecg-amplitude` and
  `--motion-sensor-gain`.
